---

## Role purpose

The AI Engineer designs, builds and runs AI systems that work reliably in production. The role sits at the intersection of software engineering, machine learning and cloud infrastructure: taking a business problem, selecting the right approach (generative, agentic, or classical ML), and delivering it as a secure, observable, cost-controlled system that real users depend on.

This is a builder's role. Success is measured by solutions that reach production and hold up there — not by prototypes.

---

## Key responsibilities

### Solution design and delivery
- Translate business requirements into technical designs, working with product owners, domain experts and client stakeholders.
- Build end-to-end AI applications: retrieval and grounding pipelines, agentic workflows with tool use and function calling, and integrations into enterprise systems (CRM, ERP, data platforms, internal APIs).
- Select appropriately between foundation models, fine-tuning, retrieval augmentation and classical ML, and be able to justify the choice on cost, latency, accuracy and risk.

### Engineering quality
- Write clean, tested, maintainable code and participate in code review.
- Build and maintain data pipelines that feed model training, evaluation and inference.
- Apply cloud-native patterns — containerisation, CI/CD, infrastructure as code, serverless and microservices — on at least one major cloud platform.

### Evaluation and reliability
- Design evaluation frameworks and datasets that measure accuracy, groundedness and task success, and report results on a regular cadence.
- Instrument systems for observability: tracing, logging, prompt and model versioning, drift detection, latency and cost monitoring.
- Diagnose and engineer around AI-specific failure modes — hallucination, retrieval gaps, context overflow, agent looping, tool-call errors, latency spikes.

### Responsible AI, security and privacy
- Implement guardrails, access controls and content safety measures proportionate to the use case.
- Handle training, test and production data in line with data protection obligations and client confidentiality requirements.
- Document model behaviour, limitations and known risks to support governance and audit.

### Collaboration and growth
- Explain design choices and trade-offs clearly to non-technical stakeholders.
- Contribute to reusable accelerators, internal standards and shared tooling.
- Mentor junior engineers and keep pace with a fast-moving tooling landscape.

## Required qualifications

- Bachelor's degree in Computer Science, Software Engineering, Data Science, Mathematics or a related quantitative field — or equivalent demonstrated experience.
- 3+ years in software engineering, data engineering or machine learning, including at least 1–2 years building AI/ML applications that reached production.
- Strong Python; solid grounding in software engineering fundamentals (version control, testing, API design, code review).
- Hands-on experience with LLM integration: prompt design, retrieval-augmented generation, vector stores, evaluation, and orchestration frameworks (for example LangChain, LangGraph, Semantic Kernel or equivalent).
- Working knowledge of at least one cloud AI platform (Azure AI, AWS Bedrock/SageMaker, or Google Cloud Vertex AI).
- Familiarity with SQL and data modelling; comfort working with messy real-world data.
- Ability to communicate technical reasoning clearly in written and spoken English.

---

## Preferred

- Experience with MLOps/LLMOps tooling and CI/CD for model deployment.
- Exposure to classical ML frameworks (PyTorch, TensorFlow, scikit-learn, XGBoost) and to NLP or computer vision problems.
- Experience in a regulated environment (financial services, healthcare, public sector) or with model risk management.
- Cloud or AI certifications (for example Azure AI Engineer Associate, AWS Machine Learning Specialty, Google Cloud ML Engineer).
- Front-end exposure (React/TypeScript) for building demos and internal tools.
- Client-facing or consulting experience.

---

## Attributes we look for

- **Pragmatic** — chooses the simplest approach that solves the problem.
- **Evidence-driven** — measures before claiming improvement.
- **Adaptable** — comfortable with ambiguity and with tooling that changes every few months.
- **Accountable** — takes ownership of outcomes, not just tickets.
- **Candid** — honest about model limitations, especially with stakeholders who expect magic.

## What the Big Four emphasise

Common threads across current Deloitte, PwC, EY and KPMG postings for this role:

- **Production over experimentation.** KPMG frames the job as bridging experimental AI and enterprise production through scalable, secure agentic workflows with measurable ROI. EY describes the AI Engineer as the core builder on the delivery team, responsible for AI/ML pipelines, agentic workflows, evaluation frameworks, observability and governance.
- **GenAI and agentic work is now central**, with classical ML still present. Deloitte asks engineers to combine non-deterministic LLMs with deterministic software engineering to build GenAI applications.
- **Evaluation and observability are named responsibilities**, not afterthoughts. Deloitte expects engineers to build evaluation frameworks for model accuracy and report on it regularly; its NZ posting lists LLMOps work including automated evaluations, tracing, prompt versioning, safety redteaming and cost/latency optimisation.
- **Responsible AI sits inside the engineering job.** EY looks for awareness of ethical AI principles, compliance frameworks and guardrails for sensitive use cases.
- **Business framing matters.** PwC leads with applying data, algorithms and software engineering to build and deploy systems that create AI and ML solutions at scale in service of decision-making.
